Great crested newt
Triturus cristatus
▾
Female (top), breeding male (middle), male underside (bottom). Illustrations © field guide source — personal use only.
Size14–17 cm
CrestLarge jagged dorsal crest with a gap at the tail base
TailSilver-white flash stripe along tail flanks
BellyVivid orange-yellow with large irregular black spots
SkinDark grey-black, coarsely granular — almost warty
CloacalSwollen black cloacal gland visible in breeding season
Size15–18 cm (larger than male)
CrestNo crest — low dorsal ridge only; orange stripe along lower tail edge
BellyOrange with black spots, less vivid than male
SkinDark grey-black, granular
ThroatPale with dark spots; distinctive pale throat midline
Size4–8 cm after metamorphosis
BellyOrange-yellow belly already visible — key feature
SkinGranular dark skin, no crest
NoteFound on land around pond margins Jul–Sep. Tiny but belly pattern distinctive
⚠️ Fully protected. Handle only under licence (WCA 1981 & Habitats Regs 2017).

Lissotriton vulgaris
▾
Female (top), breeding male (right), male underside (bottom). Illustrations © field guide source — personal use only.
Size8–11 cm
CrestContinuous wavy crest from head to tail tip; spotted with dark marks
BellyOrange-yellow with round dark spots extending onto throat
ThroatSpotted — key difference from palmate male
SkinSmooth olive-brown above; fine dark spots on flanks
TailFlattened laterally; fine filament tip
Size8–11 cm
CrestNo crest; low dorsal ridge
BellyPale orange with spots
ThroatSpotted — distinguishes from palmate female
SkinOlive to light brown; two faint dorsal stripes visible from above
Size3–6 cm
BellyPale with fine dark speckling
ThroatShows some spotting
NoteVery similar to juvenile palmate — throat spotting is best guide
Palmate newt
Lissotriton helveticus
▾
Female (left), breeding male (right), male underside (centre). Illustrations © field guide source — personal use only.
Size7–9 cm (smallest UK newt)
FeetWebbed hind feet in breeding season — key ID feature
TailEnds in a distinct dark filament tip
CrestLow, straight-edged — much less prominent than smooth
ThroatPlain unspotted pinkish — key difference from smooth male
BellyPale yellow-orange, few spots
Size7–9 cm
ThroatPlain unspotted pale/pink — best separation from smooth female
BellyPale yellow to cream, lightly spotted
SkinOlive-brown; often faint lateral stripe
TailShort, blunt-ended — no filament
Size3–5 cm
ThroatPlain pale — most reliable separation from juvenile smooth
BellyPale, lightly marked
NoteVery similar to juvenile smooth. Throat spotting is primary guide
Common frog
Rana temporaria
▾
Adult common frog showing key features. © source — personal use only.
Size6–9 cm
SnoutPointed — key difference from common toad
SkinSmooth; highly variable colour (green, brown, grey, yellow, even pink)
FlankPale cream/yellow lateral fold along each side
EyeBrown patch behind eye; dark bars on hind legs
MoveHops — toads tend to walk/crawl
SpawnRound clumps in shallow water, Feb–Mar
Size1–3 cm at metamorphosis
SkinOften greenish-brown; smooth
NoteTiny froglets emerge Jun–Aug, often in huge numbers
IDPointed snout and lateral fold already visible at small size
Common toad
Bufo bufo
▾
Male common toad. © Gemma Jane Fairchild — personal use only.
Size6–13 cm; females much larger than males
SnoutRounded/blunt — key difference from common frog
SkinDry, warty texture; olive-brown to grey
EyesCopper/orange iris; horizontal pupil
ParotoidProminent raised parotoid glands behind eyes — toxic secretion
MoveWalks and crawls; rarely hops
SpawnLong strings in double rows around pond vegetation, Mar–Apr
Size1–2 cm at metamorphosis
SkinWarty texture already visible; dark brown
NoteEmerge from ponds in large numbers May–Jun
IDBlunt snout and warty skin separate from juvenile frogs
Natterjack toad
Epidalea calamita
▾
Adult natterjack toad showing yellow dorsal stripe. © source — personal use only.
Size6–8 cm
StripeBright yellow dorsal stripe from head to vent — unmistakable
SkinWarty; green-grey with darker blotches; often yellowish tinge
LegsShort hind legs — runs rather than hops (mouse-like gait)
EyesGreen iris; horizontal pupil
CallLoud rasping call audible up to 2km on warm evenings
SpawnSingle or double row strings in shallow warm water, Apr–Jun
Size0.8–1.5 cm at metamorphosis — tiny
StripeYellow dorsal stripe visible even at very small size — key feature
NoteEmerge en masse; can be found in huge numbers at breeding sites
StatusProtected species — survey licence required for handling
⚠️ Protected species. Handle only under licence (WCA 1981).
